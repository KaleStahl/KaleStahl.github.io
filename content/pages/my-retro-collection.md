# Storing Decks
Building decks is super fun, but once you're done you have to find somewhere to put your finished product. Despite each card being relatively small, somehow storing my Pokemon card collection takes a lot of time and energy; especially with my ever-growing collection of retro decks. In the following sections I will lay out what I currently do to store and build my retro collection, and also provide options for things I have tried. Retro Pokemon decks can be rather expensive, so I recommend you take the proper precautions to protect and store them.

## Sleeves
This is probably the most important way to protect your cards. Any self-respecting card game player knows how important it is to sleeve your cards before playing. Good sleeves help the cards shuffle better, protect the cards, and help you to avoid marked cards in your deck. Since I still actively play the game at locals and regionals, I usually just sleeve my retro decks in sleeves that are too old or dirty for tournament play. This works fine, and doesn't involve spending extra money on sleeves. If you don't have a surplus of excess sleeves like I do, then I will outline some of the best options.

- The most popular brand among competitive Pokemon players is [Dragon Shield](https://www.dragonshield.com/). These are usually my go-tos when I enter a tournament, so most of my retro decks are sleeved in old Dragon Shields. Their color variety is unmatched (Except for limes. BRING BACK LIME), and they have a variety of options between Matte, Dual-Matte, and classic style sleeves. Matte's are my go to for tournaments, but most colors have somewhat translucent backs, so if you have World Cards or cards with alternate backs you want to go for Dual Matte's which are thicker but have a fully opaque back. Recently Dragon Shield has had major issues with quality control in color and manufacturing of sleeves. Sleeve color can vary widely between boxes of the same color, or some boxes have many unusable sleeves. This makes it so those 40 extra sleeves are unable to be combined with another box of the same color without marking sleeves. They are also a bit on the pricy side, so spending $12 or more on a box of sleeves for a deck can get expensive if you are looking to build a large collection like I have. I also find that Dragon Shields stop shuffling well much before others, so if your deck is used often you may want to go with a cheaper or more durable option.
- My new go-to sleeves for tournaments have been from [End Game](https://endgametcg.ca/). They are a fairly new Canadian company so finding their sleeves in stores is a little hard, but for sleeves you can get online, they are great. Color consistency is really good over multiple boxes so I can buy 3 boxes of one color to sleeve up 5 individual decks, which I can never do with Dragon Shields. They also shuffle well for at lot longer than Dragon Shields, which makes them great for reusing on retro decks. They don't have many colors and shipping can be expensive, but I really like these so far.
- The last option are cheap Chinese sleeves usually sold on Amazon. I don't like buying from Amazon, but sometimes it is the only option if you don't want to spend a lot sleeving your decks. I have actually liked using [these sleeves](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DP6SSP6N?ref=ppx_yo2ov_dt_b_fed_asin_title&th=1). They are fully opaque and come out to around $2 per deck if you buy them in bulk, which is not bad. They also don't feel horrible when shuffling, which is not something I can say for all sleeves. I have also bought [these sleeves](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CLHTM6QM?ref=ppx_yo2ov_dt_b_fed_asin_title&th=1). They are extremely thin and feel super plasticky to shuffle, but they aren't unusable if you don't really care about sleeve quality and just want a bunch of sleeves to use.

## Deck Boxes
Deckboxes are essential if you want to keep your decks together for any period of time (Trust me, piles NEVER end well) and let you take them places without losing any cards. When going to tournaments, I usually just carry around a standard size deckbox with a folding velcro top. These are great, especially since there are so many official designs sold by Pokemon Center. 

They are not my first choice for retro decks however, since I don't always know which deck is in which box, and labelling the boxes is hard because you don't want to cover the nice artwork you bought the box for in the first place. Enter one of my favorite products ever: [Burger Tokens Perfect Fit Deckboxes](https://burgertokens.com/products/perfect-fit-deckboxes). These took me like a year of searching to find after seeing one random person from Japan post a picture of all their decks in them on Twitter. After all that work, finding these have definitely been worth it. I print custom labels, and these being the most barebones, blank slate deckboxes is perfect for this. They also are much smaller than standard deckboxes, so if you have a large collecton they fit nicely in any box that can fit cards. Unfortunately they don't provide much protection and are prone to breaking if haphazardly thrown in a bag, so take that into account when choosing which deckboxes to use.

I have also seen many people use the standard [Ultra Pro Deckboxes](https://www.amazon.com/Ultra-Pro-Magic-Pokemon-YuGiOh/dp/B074DVF48M/?_encoding=UTF8&pd_rd_w=UbNZm&content-id=amzn1.sym.ddb3f609-e1e5-4e5d-96f6-88c55733f70a&pf_rd_p=ddb3f609-e1e5-4e5d-96f6-88c55733f70a&pf_rd_r=3HD1RM3G08M67TW0CT9Y&pd_rd_wg=V9pF4&pd_rd_r=486f6aff-fa41-4733-918e-f1507a824015&ref_=pd_hp_d_r_atf_sspa_mw_gateway&ie=UTF8&sp_csd=d2lkZ2V0TmFtZT1zcF9hdGZfd3BfcGhvbmVfaG9tZXBhZ2U&th=1) since they are pretty cheap and also have a small footprint. They also have pre-printed areas to label the boxes without a label maker, which could be nice if you don't love printers as much as I do. They are also in almost any card store, so you won't have to order them online.

## Storage Boxes
You probably don't need these until you have a bunch of decks built, but I am a degenerate and have too many. BCW makes probably the best selection of trading card storage accessories. Unfortunately for some (including me), they appear to only sell from Amazon, so that is where I link to here. I've seen their products at local game stores sometimes, but the most reliable place to get them is Amazon.

 Right now, I'm using these [3-row Boxes](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B073513HH6?ref_=ppx_hzsearch_conn_dt_b_fed_asin_title_2&th=1) from BCW for my decks. The fit 21 perfect fit deckboxes and 4 of them fit perfectly into one of the boxes in my Ikea Kallax shelves. They also have white labels if you want to label them from the front, and even have card inserts so you can organize the rows. They are a bit expensive, but I have found them to be durable and high quality compared to other cardboard boxes. 

If you don't use my favorite Perfect Fits, I've seen people use the tried and true [4 Row Card Boxes](https://www.amazon.com/BCW-3200-Card-Monster-Storage/dp/B09KJ4915L/ref=sr_1_6?dib=eyJ2IjoiMSJ9.v_zNIBwUMPpPPGKJTESgKT7mpyx5P6GsicEYPHebOmAe0A1mXC8QU6vOkX9kMvpNQkGixlxFoD7ejfk-vCyUCqBMGqigUV3fVW3SeiarCTfpUY-r9wvSyeb70tiXctmCF11fdME00Q67yzW0bR-qCqHAp-UJEEZ6Se_wWqnLvPT4ulQHZpN6aoZwh5YDNVw6mMnejiu-cP0Pj9Q_ppO6uYYlbLiA_RidBuDsHBxvMtOd9M24ldSfKZKJipFnmtNat4h6YmH-qFglpYlL2vB3qg8CVGeaLio-UcjLme6Mnss.LEAh-Jpr1iEusYn-M7_1IgSje5WE3cjQJb5fRP4PUQI&dib_tag=se&keywords=BCW+bulk+box&qid=1777328015&sbo=RZvfv%2F%2FHxDF%2BO5021pAnSA%3D%3D&sr=8-6) to hold like 30 decks in the Ultra Pro deckboxes. I've found these boxes to be super bulky and not very durable, so this is definitely a good option if you don't plan to take your decks a lot of places.

If you really want to splurge, BCW also has plastic versions of the [4 Row Card Boxes](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1S7Z9P4?ref_=ppx_hzsearch_conn_dt_b_fed_asin_title_6&th=1). I use a couple of these to organize my bulk cards, and they are great for that since they stack nicely and have built in divider tabs. I used the carboard boxes for the longest time, but they started to degrade over a few years so I upgraded and do not regret it. I don't use these for my retro decks currently, but they do comfortably fit even an official size deckbox, so I'm sure they are another good premium option.
